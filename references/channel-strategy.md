# Phase 0 (first): format, audience and channel strategy for a puppet series

Part of the general `ai-puppet-video` skill. It works for **any** puppet cast, language or market. Decide this **before** designing characters (0a), because the format and audience change the character design, the bible, the episode length and the editing.

**Source:** `/Users/dribrahimm/1-AI-Influncer/researches/YouTube Puppet Channel Strategies.md`, a 2026 desk study of 26 puppet channels in 8 languages.
- Subscriber, view and revenue figures in it are third-party tracker **estimates**. Use them for direction only, never as promises.
- Re-check any platform rule (COPPA labels, Shorts features, merch terms) before relying on it; platforms change.

**Why puppets:** a physical puppet is a "live-action VTuber".
- It is an instant visual hook in the first seconds of a scroll.
- It keeps the creator anonymous and never ages.
- It is a 3-D IP that can become merchandise (plush and replicas).

AI-generated felt puppets inherit the hook. They also inherit two risks: getting the **"made for kids" audience setting** wrong and **third-party IP**.

## 1. Pick ONE strategic archetype

| # | Archetype | What it is | Engine to copy (mechanics, not content) | [Example] channels in the study |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Live-action VTuber / armchair analyst | The puppet hosts commentary in a high-CPM niche (games, tech, science, finance) | The puppet treats its own illusion as real and keeps running lore. Long-form cuts back to the puppet every 20–40 s for a gag or reaction over B-roll. A two-speed ecosystem: polished essays plus fast daily rants on a second channel. | Arlo / TopicArlo, Biología desde cero |
| 2 | Unscripted sharp-tongue interviewer | A blunt puppet MC interviews real human experts or guests | The puppet can say truths a human host can't. Almost no script, then aggressive jump cuts that trim every slow line, with fast subtitles (reported about 70% average retention). | Yurindo's R.B. Bukkoro, Giant PengTV |
| 3 | Domestic sitcom / "PlushTuber" universe | A recurring cast in an ordinary home; fast situational comedy | A straight-man character plus a chaotic, catchphrase-heavy breakout character. Titles and thumbnails tie each episode to a current trend. | SML (after 2021), CrazyMarioBros, Sonic and Friends |
| 4 | "Brain-rest" / healing micro-skit | Very short vertical vignettes built on one vocal or physical gimmick | Low cognitive load: no forced climax (whisper "healing"), **or** loud chaos with one screamed catchphrase. A simple repeatable dance or gesture fans copy (UGC). Minimal dialogue plus subtitles for cross-border reach. | Puppet Sunsun, Shark Puppet |
| 5 | Stand-up, satire and celebrity podcast | The puppet as an adult comedian, satirist or talk host | Free full specials plus crowdwork Shorts that sell tickets. | 6 | Prestige lore and edutainment | Fewer, high-craft episodes with sets, songs and hidden lore | Meta-lore and background clues feed "explained" and theory videos, which send viewers back. A cheaper solo-host spin-off keeps the upload cadence. | Don't Hug Me I'm Scared, Puppet History |
| 7 | Preschool pedagogy and music | Slow "parentese", songs and repetition for toddlers | Licensing, apps and toys, not ad revenue. **Made for Kids** by design. | Sesame Street, Ms. Rachel, Ahlan Simsim |

**Rules**

- **One channel, one format, one audience promise.** The recommendation algorithm rewards single-format consistency. Puppet History grew after moving to its own channel. Put rants, behind-the-scenes or streams on a second channel once the main one has traction.
- **Choose the archetype with the user** and write it at the top of the series bible (`series-bible.md` §3 "Format"). Archetypes can be hybrids. **[Example]** The first project is archetype 3, a domestic sitcom with an adult cast and an everyday-life flaw. Its silent-comedy episodes behave like archetype 4 for Shorts reach.
- **The AI-video pipeline in this skill fits archetypes 3, 4 and 6 best:** scripted, short, visual comedy. Archetypes 2 and 5 depend on real unscripted humans or live crowds, which AI generation does not replace.

## 2. Audience setting: classify honestly, then design for the audience you actually want

**Official rule** ([YouTube: Determining if your content is made for kids](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076?hl=en)):
- "Made for kids" is not only preschool. In the US a child is anyone under 13 (other countries may differ). Content is made for kids when children are its primary audience, **or one of its audiences** ("mixed audience" counts as made for kids too).
- Every creator must set the audience for the channel or each video. YouTube ties this to its FTC agreement and COPPA. It says the accurate label is the creator's legal responsibility, and that you should not rely on YouTube's systems to set it for you, because they may miss content the FTC treats as child-directed.
- A wrong setting can bring consequences on YouTube and legal consequences under COPPA and other laws.
- YouTube weighs the **actual** audience and content, not a disclaimer. Its factors:
  - subject matter (for example early education),
  - the intended audience (including metadata) or the actual audience, and other evidence such as data,
  - child actors or models,
  - characters, celebrities or toys that appeal to children, including animated or cartoon characters,
  - language meant for children to understand,
  - activities that appeal to children (play-acting, simple songs or games, early education),
  - songs, stories or poems for children,
  - whether the content is advertised to children.
- Not made for kids: content with mature themes, and **general-audience** content. Animation that appeals to everyone is general audience. A video is not made for kids just because it is family-friendly, shows an activity associated with children, or children might see it by chance.

**Features off on made-for-kids content** ([YouTube: features affected](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654?hl=en)): personalised ads, comments, the notification bell, cards and end screens, channel memberships, merchandise and ticketing, the miniplayer, saving to playlists and Watch Later, autoplay on Home, Super Chat and Super Stickers, live chat, the donate button, video watermarks. A channel set as made for kids also loses posts.

**Research claims** (the study, not YouTube; use for direction only):
- Creators report a 60–90% RPM drop without personalised ads.
- The study says YouTube's automated systems can flag puppet videos as kids' content because puppets look like children's TV.
- Two puppet channels in the study declined or rebranded after drifting into kids' content.
- Several plush channels in the study add a "13+" line to the description. **Do not copy this as a fix:** a disclaimer does not change what the content is or who it is for.

**What to do in production**

- **Classify every video honestly** by its actual intended audience and content, using the factors above. Re-check each video, not only the channel.
- **Puppets alone do not make a video child-directed, and muted colours or an adult topic alone do not make it adult.** Weigh the whole video: topic, language, humour, activities, songs, characters, pacing, title, thumbnail, and who watches.
- **Never choose "Not made for kids" to keep comments, personalised ads, merch, end screens or a pinned sponsor comment.** That is mislabelling.
- **If the series is genuinely for adults or a general audience**, make that true in the work itself:
  - topics: adult situations (work, marriage, money, bureaucracy),
  - humour: wit, irony, adult embarrassment, not slapstick aimed at young children,
  - pacing and language: adult conversational speed, no nursery-rhyme rhythm, no baby talk, no alphabet or colour lessons, no simple sing-along songs,
  - design: puppets that portray adults, no toddler proportions in wardrobe and props, no toy-box styling, no toddler cues in thumbnails and titles,
  - promotion: never advertise or pitch it to children.
- **If the series (or a video) is child-directed**, including older children and not only preschool, set it as made for kids and plan money around that from the start: contextual (non-personalised) ads, licensing, apps, toys and books, sponsorships that do not need personalised ads, comments, links in end screens or a pinned comment. Archetype 7 is this case by design.
- **When unsure**, change the content until the answer is clear, or ask a lawyer. YouTube says it cannot give legal advice and points unsure creators to legal counsel.
- **In the bible:** write who the puppets portray and who the series is for, with the reasons from the factors above (`series-bible.md` §3 "Audience"). For an adult series, add the toddler-aesthetic list above to the avoid list.
- **In character design (0a):** for an adult series, state "adult … not a child, infant or toddler" in every master prompt (`characters.md` §2).

## 3. Own 100% of the IP

- **Never build the cast on third-party characters** (game, film or toy characters), even as parody or "plush" versions. The study's biggest case: a 13-year channel deleted after a cease-and-desist, rebuilt on fully original puppets, and now selling its own replica puppets and plush.
- This matches the skill's originality rules:
  - adapt mechanics, never copy (`idea-from-reference.md`),
  - no character copying from reference channels (`series-bible.md` §2),
  - text-free, brand-free props.
- Record provenance for every character image (`characters.md` §6, `generation_provenance`) so ownership is documented.

## 4. Design implications for the characters (feeds 0a)

- **Readable at thumbnail size, in 0.5 s on a phone.** Give each character one bold, distinct colour and silhouette that stands out against a dark or busy thumbnail. Make the face expressive (big eyes, a clear brow) so a single emotion reads instantly. This is the same "distinct at a small thumbnail size" rule already in `characters.md` §1 (supporting-cast pattern).
- **One signature gimmick per character, vocal and/or physical.** **[Example]** a whisper voice, a screamed catchphrase, a "creepy-cute" look. Keep at least one **silent visual** catchphrase, because this pipeline's video is SFX-only (`series-bible.md` §6).
- **Merch-able shape.** A clean, simple silhouette and a signature item make a plush or replica plausible later. It is a bonus, never a reason to compromise the comedy.
- **Persona lore:** the character "believes" it is real and has consistent small lore (where it lives, what it likes). Keep it in `character.json → persona` and label it PROPOSED until approved.

## 5. Episode and editing implications (feeds phases 1–2 and 8)

- **Shorts first:** in several channels in the study, Shorts drive 85–92% of recent views. Design every episode so its key beat survives as a 7–60 s vertical Short:
  - a visual hook in the first second,
  - a payoff that reads on mute,
  - a clean button for the loop.
- **Tighten like an unscripted show:** cut every slow beat and dead hold. Fast subtitles help retention and cross-language reach. This skill's own "dead tail" and "listener freeze" rules point the same way.
- **Long-form from Shorts:** bundle related episodes into a numbered 10–15 min compilation. Long sitcom episodes cut back to the most expressive puppet reaction often.
- **Titles:** a curiosity gap, and for sitcoms a link to a current trend (`[Character]'s <trend>!`). Never a generic label. No third-party names in a way that implies affiliation.
- **Thumbnails:** clean negative space, one high-contrast expressive puppet face, one clear emotion.

## 6. Real-puppetry craft rules worth copying into AI prompts and QC

These come from solo puppeteers' monitor-puppetry practice. They also describe what makes an AI puppet look alive or dead.

| Craft rule | Use in this pipeline |
|---|---|
| Eye focus is everything: a puppet whose eyes tilt slightly upward looks lifeless | QC every shot's eyeline: eyes on the scene partner or object, never drifting up or into the lens (`qc-checklist.md`). In Blender previs, key the gaze explicitly (`blender-previs.md` §3). |
| Camera at the puppet's eye level | Default to eye-level framing. Low or high angles are deliberate choices (`cinematography.md`). |
| Soft, diffused, wrap-around light, no hard shadows from nose or brow onto the mouth | Prompt "soft diffused light that wraps the felt"; reject panels or clips with hard nose shadows over the mouth. |
| The jaw opens on vowels, not random flapping | For dialogue: animate the Blender stand-in mouth on the vowels of the recorded or planned line, and check the AI jaw windows against them (`voice-script.md` §B, "Open: dialogue on the Blender route"). |
| The character voice sits slightly higher or more textured than the performer's own | Choose one fixed voice-changer preset per character that clearly separates them. Record it in the cast file (`voice-script.md` §D). |
| Intercut the puppet with B-roll in long-form | The puppet host cuts back every 20–40 s. Use cutaways to inserts and props for pacing. |

## 7. Pre-launch checklist (when the series becomes a real channel)

1. Archetype chosen and written into the bible, with one format per channel.
2. Audience classified honestly with YouTube's factors (§2), for the channel and for each video. Content made to match that label (an adult series has adult topics, humour, pacing and design and no toddler cues; a child-directed series is labelled made for kids, with monetization planned for it). No "13+" disclaimer used as a substitute.
3. Every character original and documented: masters, provenance, `character.json`.
4. A Shorts-first episode template: hook ≤ 1 s, mute-readable payoff, button, subtitles.
5. A thumbnail and title template tested at phone size.
6. A second channel planned for off-format content, opened only after traction.
7. Merch is optional and later. A crowdfunded plush drop is a common first step at about 10K–100K subscribers in the study; never before the community exists.

**[Example]** A cast-specific skill for one series (for instance the Professor and Nora's own channel) would fix the archetype, audience line, title and thumbnail templates and the character gimmicks here once, and reuse them every episode. This general file stays the decision guide.
